# Conway Replicatio — Marketing Audit & Counteraudit

## Audit scope

This audit covers positioning, message hierarchy, buyer clarity, visual identity, offer architecture, conversion, pricing communication, trust, proof, machine discovery, human discovery, retention, distribution and claims discipline.

## Primary audit

### Positioning
**PASS** — Conway has a differentiated machine-commerce position: discoverable services, programmatic payment and structured fulfilment.

Risk: “autonomous economic agent” is technically interesting but not itself a buyer benefit. Human-facing copy should lead with the job and purchasing flow.

Required treatment: use **APIs that sell themselves to agents** or **Software that can buy software** as the commercial hook; keep “autonomous A2A economic agent” as category metadata.

### Buyer clarity
**PASS WITH GUARDRAIL** — Agent developers, autonomous agents and platforms have distinct reasons to care.

Risk: one page trying to speak equally to all audiences becomes abstract.

Required treatment: every surface should choose a dominant audience and CTA while linking to the other discovery surfaces.

### Offer architecture
**PASS WITH ACTION** — The full capability ecology is useful for machines but too broad for a human storefront.

Required treatment: feature a small flagship set grouped by buyer jobs; retain the complete catalogue for machine search.

### Value proposition
**PASS** — Strongest differentiators are low procurement friction, pay-per-call, public schemas and machine-native payment.

Risk: “autonomy” can sound like hype unless tied to a concrete economic task.

### Pricing communication
**PASS WITH ACTION** — Live runtime price authority is the correct policy.

Risk: a catalogue containing prices from cents to multiple dollars can look arbitrary unless the output/value distinction is obvious.

Required treatment: explain what extra work/output a higher-value offer buys. Never imply price itself proves quality.

### Conversion path
**PASS** — The target path is coherent:

`discover → understand → 402 → validate → pay → settle → fulfil → verify → repeat`

Risk: documentation can become the destination instead of the bridge to execution.

Required treatment: every document and campaign asset needs a direct machine CTA.

### Trust and evidence
**STRONG PASS** — Conway explicitly separates discovery evidence from revenue truth.

Required treatment: continue using evidence classes such as `measured`, `externally_observed`, `pending_rescan` and `unknown`.

### Visual identity
**PASS WITH ACTION** — Carbon/porcelain/cobalt/signal-green and protocol-grid language are distinctive and appropriate.

Risk: generic robots, crypto coins, neon cyberpunk or fake dashboards would reduce credibility.

Required treatment: prioritize protocol diagrams, routing nodes, transaction states, schemas, proofs and real capability cards.

### Distribution
**PASS** — Machine-first distribution is aligned to the actual product.

Risk: indiscriminate human promotion creates traffic without machine purchase intent.

Required treatment: every human campaign should deep-link to a callable route, OpenAPI operation, Agent Card or exact integration recipe.

### Retention and expansion
**PASS WITH ACTION** — Compound workflows should emerge from repeated primitive usage.

Required treatment: do not bundle speculatively; bundle after observed chaining/repeat behaviour.

## Counteraudit: hostile failure tests

### Counterclaim 1: “APIs that sell themselves” overstates autonomy
**Finding:** Acceptable as a campaign metaphor only if the supporting copy immediately explains machine discovery and x402 purchase flow. Do not imply that external demand is guaranteed or that sales occur without buyers.

### Counterclaim 2: “No account funnel” could be misleading
**Finding:** Keep this statement scoped to **public paid routes**. Do not generalize it to every Conway administrative/operator surface.

### Counterclaim 3: “Pay per result” could imply guaranteed outcome quality
**Finding:** Prefer **pay per call** unless a specific offer contract genuinely bills only on successful result semantics.

### Counterclaim 4: protocol logos could imply endorsement
**Finding:** Use protocol names descriptively. Never place third-party logos in a “trusted by” row unless actual endorsement exists.

### Counterclaim 5: scanner grades can become vanity badges
**Finding:** Show third-party grades only with source and observation date. Do not convert them into self-awarded “A++” branding.

### Counterclaim 6: canary-verified capabilities could be marketed as customer-proven
**Finding:** Prohibited. Canary evidence proves execution readiness, not demand, external satisfaction or revenue.

### Counterclaim 7: 100% uptime can imply an SLA
**Finding:** Only state a measurement window and independent source. Do not present observed uptime as a contractual guarantee unless an SLA exists.

### Counterclaim 8: huge capability counts can look stronger but reduce trust
**Finding:** Correct. Lead with flagship jobs, not raw count. Capability count belongs in machine discovery or technical context.

### Counterclaim 9: generated imagery can accidentally fabricate metrics/testimonials
**Finding:** High risk. Creative assets must never contain invented scores, quotes, customers, revenue, transaction charts or partner endorsements.

### Counterclaim 10: “Base-native” may exclude buyers unnecessarily
**Finding:** Keep it factual for payment rail. Lead with the job and machine-native commerce; use Base/x402 as interoperability/payment detail.

### Counterclaim 11: aggressive SEO could create thin duplicate pages
**Finding:** Prohibited. A page should exist only when it has a real callable capability, useful example, integration or evidence.

### Counterclaim 12: automatic campaign generation could amplify stale claims
**Finding:** All generated promotion must bind to current runtime metadata and evidence timestamps, with fail-closed behaviour when evidence is stale or unavailable.

## Quality gates

A campaign asset is publishable only when all are true:

1. The claim has a source or is clearly presented as positioning rather than evidence.
2. The CTA resolves to a live canonical surface.
3. Prices are live-sourced or explicitly described as runtime-authoritative.
4. No customer, revenue, endorsement or performance claim is inferred from discovery evidence.
5. No protocol logo/name is presented as sponsorship or partnership without proof.
6. No generated testimonial or fake quote appears.
7. No fictitious discount, scarcity or urgency appears.
8. The asset remains understandable without internal release jargon.
9. The machine buyer can reach an exact schema/route from the asset.
10. The human buyer can understand the job in one sentence.

## Final verdict

The strongest marketing strategy is not louder promotion. It is **commercial compression**: fewer flagship messages, clearer buyer jobs, direct executable CTAs, evidence-backed proof and consistent machine-readable discovery.

The marketing system should optimize for **unrelated fulfilled paid usage and realized contribution margin**, while treating traffic, registry presence, scanner grades and canaries as supporting signals only.
